# Voice, language & typography (Hebrew)

How to make Hebrew copy read native, plus RTL/typography hygiene.

## Register ladder (most casual → most formal)
1. **TikTok / IG Reels / Stories** — spoken Hebrew, slang, contractions, direct
   "you". e.g. "סבבה", "אש", "וואלה", "תכלס", "בא לי", "על הפנים", "שורף".
2. **Instagram feed / Facebook** — friendly conversational, light slang.
3. **LinkedIn / B2B** — clean professional Hebrew, minimal slang, Eng. tech terms
   ok (סטארטאפ, פיצ'ר, דדליין).
4. **Official / legal / government** — formal, no slang. (Rarely needed here.)

## Native vs. translated tells
Avoid these "translationese" markers:
- Calqued English idioms ("בסוף היום" for *at the end of the day* — prefer "בסופו
  של דבר" / "בשורה התחתונה").
- Over-literal "אתה יכול ל..." stacks — Hebrew prefers imperative: "תתחילו".
- English sentence rhythm and over-long sentences. Hebrew social copy is punchy.
- Wrong preposition collocations — verify verb+preposition pairs sound natural.

## Gender (critical)
Hebrew verbs/adjectives/pronouns are gendered. Decide the addressee and commit:
- **Single male:** "תירשם", "מוכן?"
- **Single female:** "תירשמי", "מוכנה?"
- **Mixed / crowd (default masculine plural):** "תירשמו", "מוכנים?"
- **Gender-neutral options:** rephrase to nouns/infinitives ("להירשם עכשיו"),
  or address the plural. State which choice you made if it matters to the brand.
When the brand has a defined persona/audience gender, follow it consistently
across the whole piece — don't switch mid-post.

## Slang — use with care
Match slang to audience age and platform. A partial bank (casual→strong):
- סבבה / אחלה / וואלה / תכלס / בקטנה / יאללה / חבל על הזמן / פצצה / אש / שורף /
  על הפנים (negative) / גועלי (negative) / מהמם / טירוף / חד משמעית.
Don't overload — one or two per post. Never put strong slang on B2B/LinkedIn.

## RTL & typography hygiene
- Output Hebrew as plain right-to-left text; let the renderer handle direction.
- **Mixed content:** Latin brand names, URLs, @handles, prices, and numbers sit
  inside RTL text — keep them intact; don't reverse characters manually.
- **Punctuation:** end Hebrew sentences with the period/!/? on the correct (left)
  side — write naturally and don't hand-place bidi marks unless something renders
  wrong.
- **Numbers:** Hebrew uses Western digits (0–9), read left-to-right within RTL.
- **Currency:** ₪ (shekel) typically after the number: "99 ₪" / "₪99" both seen;
  prefer "99 ₪".
- **Emoji:** treat as accents, place where they read naturally in RTL.
- **Quotes:** Hebrew commonly uses regular quotes; gershayim (״) appear in
  acronyms (צה״ל) and geresh (׳) in transliterations — keep them.

## Hooks that work in Hebrew
- Question: "מכירים את הרגע ש...?"
- Pattern interrupt: "עצרו לשנייה."
- Number/list: "3 דברים שאף אחד לא מספר לכם על..."
- Relatable pain: "נשבר לכם מ...?"
- FOMO/time: "רק עד מחר —"

## Checklist before delivering
- [ ] Reads native, not translated.
- [ ] Gender choice consistent and intentional.
- [ ] Register fits the platform.
- [ ] No date/sensitivity conflict (see calendar.md).
- [ ] Hook in line 1; CTA present; hashtags count fits platform.
- [ ] No fabricated claims/prices — placeholders marked.
